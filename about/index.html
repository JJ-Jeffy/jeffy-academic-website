About Me

I am a first-year PhD student in the Department of Chemistry at Georgia Institute of Technology. My research interests lie at the intersection of chemistry, computational biology, and software engineering.

Research Focus

My research focuses on understanding the mechanisms of HIV-1 resistance to broadly neutralizing antibodies. I combine wet-lab experimental techniques with computational methods to investigate viral mutations and their effects on antibody binding and neutralization.

I am particularly interested in:

  • Structural biology of HIV-1 envelope glycoproteins
  • Computational modeling of protein–antibody interactions
  • Development of visualization tools for analyzing biological data
  • Application of machine learning in predicting viral resistance patterns

Background

My academic journey began at the University of Minnesota, where I earned my Bachelor’s degree in Chemistry with a minor in Computer Science. This interdisciplinary foundation allowed me to develop skills in both experimental chemistry and computational methods.

I furthered my education at UC Berkeley, completing a Master’s degree in Molecular Science and Software Engineering. This program enhanced my ability to develop computational tools for addressing complex biological problems.

Technical Skills

  • Wet Lab Techniques: Molecular cloning, protein purification, flow cytometry, cell culture, viral assays
  • Computational Methods: Molecular dynamics simulations, docking studies, QM/MM calculations
  • Programming: Python, R, JavaScript, Go, C++
  • Data Visualization: D3.js, Plotly, matplotlib
  • Web Development: Flask, React, Hugo
